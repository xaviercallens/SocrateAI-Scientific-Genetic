\section{Solutions to Selected Exercises}\label{app:solutions}

Solutions are given for the exercises whose answers are not immediate. Where an
exercise asks for a design or an argument, the solution indicates what a complete
answer must contain rather than supplying prose to copy.

\subsection*{Section 1}

\paragraph{1.1}
(a) Arithmetic --- falsified only by a computational error.
(b) Modelling --- falsified by evidence that stages interact, e.g.\ an error rate
that is not the product of per-stage factors.
(c) Measurement --- falsified by an independent determination in the same
background and reporter.
(d) Modelling --- falsified by a tumour with high polymerase error rate and low
burden, or the reverse; Section~\ref{sec:mmr} argues exactly this.

\paragraph{1.2}
A reply should make two points: that the gate establishes the conclusion follows
from the hypotheses and nothing about whether the hypotheses hold of a cell; and
that this is not a criticism of formalisation, whose value is that a hypothesis
precise enough to formalise is precise enough to refute.

\subsection*{Section 2}

\paragraph{2.1}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:gain} with $k = 1$, the ratio
$\gamma^{m+1}/\gamma^{m} = \gamma$ for every $m$. Returns are constant in the
logarithm because the stages are statistically independent and the errors
multiply; most engineered schemes share information between stages, so later
stages have less to contribute.

\paragraph{2.2}
With $\mathcal{E}_2 = \gamma^2 + c$ and $c > 0$ we can have
$\mathcal{E}_2 > \mathcal{E}_1 = \gamma$ whenever $c > \gamma - \gamma^2$. So a
two-stage enzyme is worse than a one-stage one: monotonicity fails. The
independence assumption is therefore not cosmetic --- it is what makes
``more proofreading is never worse'' true.

\paragraph{2.3}
$\gamma = e^{-\Delta\Delta G/k_BT} = 10^{-6}$ requires
$\Delta\Delta G = 6\ln 10 \approx 13.8\,k_BT$. At $310$\,K,
$k_BT \approx 2.58\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$, so
$\Delta\Delta G \approx 35.6\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. A single Watson--Crick
hydrogen bond contributes of order $5$--$10\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$, and the
difference between a correct and incorrect pair is smaller still, so no plausible
base-pairing chemistry supplies it. This is precisely why equilibrium
discrimination is insufficient and kinetic proofreading is needed.

\subsection*{Section 3}

\paragraph{3.1}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:psr1} with the ambient set $\kept$ and the subset
$\emptyset$: it gives $\errate(\kept) \le \errate(\emptyset) = \inserr$, since the
empty product is $1$. Hence PSR$_2$.

\paragraph{3.2}
(a) Multiplicativity fails: the error rate is no longer a product over
independent stages. The ceiling need not hold, and this is the one escape route
the no-go theorem leaves open.
(b) No hypothesis of Assumption~\ref{model:class} fails, but the mutant is not a
\emph{stage-removal} mutant --- $\inserr$ has changed. This is the case PSR$_3$
describes, and it is the rescue the Xing measurement forbids.
(c) The range hypothesis $\escape_i \in [0,1]$ fails, and the ceiling genuinely
fails with it: the Lean file's negative control exhibits this.

\paragraph{3.3}
A complete answer defines refutation of a family as the non-existence of any
$\theta \in \Theta$ consistent with the observation, and refutation of a member
as inconsistency for one particular $\theta$. Enlarging $\Theta$ weakens content
because the set of observations inconsistent with \emph{every} member shrinks;
in the limit of a sufficiently rich family, nothing is excluded and the family
makes no prediction.

\subsection*{Section 4}

\paragraph{4.1}
PSR$_3$ with $\rho = 2300/44 \approx 52.3$ gives
$\inserr' \ge 52.3\,\inserr$, where $\inserr$ is the exonuclease-null rate: the
insertion/extension error would have to exceed it by a factor of about $52$, and
hence exceed \emph{wild type's} by $52 \times (44/15) \approx 153$. Xing's
purified measurement forbids it, because with no repair present \texttt{P301R}
makes \emph{fewer} errors than exonuclease-null, so its intrinsic error is not
elevated at all. (Remark~\ref{rem:transport} notes the one way this rescue could
survive: if the quantity the purified assay measures is not the quantity
$\inserr$ denotes in a cell.)

\paragraph{4.2}
It establishes only that this allele is consistent with the model class --- it
does not confirm the model, because every allele that is \emph{not} a mutator is
equally consistent. A test that cannot fail for non-mutators provides no evidence
when it passes. (Compare Exercise~4.4.)

A complete answer should add the background qualification of Worked
Example~7.1: $\rho \le 1$ is a statement about the genetic background the
measurement was made in. \texttt{V411L} has $\rho = 0.42$ in MMR-proficient
yeast and is a robust mutator once MMR is removed, so ``does not break the
ceiling'' must not be read as ``has no phenotype''.

\paragraph{4.4}
If the statistic is forced to its extreme value by a symmetry of the pipeline,
then every shuffle produces a value at least as extreme, so the $p$-value is
bounded by $1/(N+1)$ regardless of the data. It measures the pipeline, not the
hypothesis. The rule violated: \emph{a control must be able to come out
non-significant}. Before reporting any test, construct the observation that would
fail it; if none exists, the test carries no evidence.

\subsection*{Section 5}

\paragraph{5.1}
$\mathrm{esc}(3, 0) = 3 \le 7 = \mathrm{esc}(7, 0)$ (neither saturated);
$\mathrm{esc}(3, 5) = 0 \le 2 = \mathrm{esc}(7, 5)$ (the mutant is fully repaired);
$\mathrm{esc}(3, 9) = 0 \le 0 = \mathrm{esc}(7, 9)$ (both fully repaired).
The ordering holds in every case, which is Theorem~\ref{thm:norev}.

\paragraph{5.2}
$\partial_p\left[p^2/(p+c)\right] = p(p + 2c)/(p+c)^2 \ge 0$ and
$\partial_c\left[p^2/(p+c)\right] = -p^2/(p+c)^2 \le 0$. Both monotonicities
hold, so Theorems~\ref{thm:norev} and~\ref{thm:forced} go through unchanged. The
conclusion depends only on the \emph{signs} of these derivatives, not on the
hard threshold, which is why the caricature is acceptable.

\paragraph{5.4}
In a null background $c = 0$ for both enzymes, so
$\mathrm{esc}(p, 0) = p$ and the comparison reduces to intrinsic production ---
which is already known from the purified assay and cannot discriminate the
hypotheses. A hypomorphic background gives $0 < c < c_{\mathrm{null}}$, where
titration (capacity consumed in proportion to load) and interference (capacity
reduced independently of load) predict different dependences of escape on $p$.

\paragraph{5.5}
Write $p = e \cdot s$, with $e$ the error rate per nucleotide incorporated and
$s$ the nucleotides the enzyme incorporates per replicated genome. Then
Theorem~\ref{thm:forced}'s hypothesis is
$e_{\mathrm{mut}} s_{\mathrm{mut}} \le e_{\mathrm{null}} s_{\mathrm{null}}$,
a constraint on the products. Xing \emph{et al.}\ measure $e$: the purified assay
reports errors per nucleotide on a defined template. Kane and Shcherbakova
measure neither factor but the \emph{outcome}, $\mathrm{esc}(p,c)$, as a fixed
mutation rate. So nothing in Section~\ref{sec:refutation} measures $s$, and
$e_{\mathrm{mut}} \le e_{\mathrm{null}}$ licenses the hypothesis only if
$s_{\mathrm{mut}} \le s_{\mathrm{null}}$ --- which hyperactivity makes
doubtful. This is the gap Remark~\ref{rem:transport} records.

\subsection*{Section 6}

\paragraph{6.1}
For $g = 4$, $\nu = 10^{-2}$: every term of
$\sum_{j=0}^{3} \nu 2^{j} 2^{3-j}$ equals $\nu 2^{3}$, so the sum is
$4\nu\cdot 2^{3} = 0.32$, and $\tfrac{1}{2}\nu g N =
\tfrac{1}{2}(10^{-2})(4)(16) = 0.32$. They agree. Note that the terms are equal
in the \emph{mean} --- each generation contributes the same expected number of
final mutants --- which is what makes the mean linear in $g$. The corresponding
terms of \eqref{eq:ldvar} are not equal, and that asymmetry is the whole content
of §\ref{sub:trap}.

\paragraph{6.4}
Both cultures have the same $g$ and $N$; by \eqref{eq:ldmean} the means are
$\tfrac12\nu_1 gN$ and $\tfrac12\nu_2 gN$, whose ratio is $\nu_2/\nu_1 = \rho$.
Every factor depending on the growth protocol cancels, which is why the ratio is
the robust statistic and the individual rates are not.

\paragraph{6.5}
A generation-$0$ event occurs in a given culture with probability $\nu =
4\times10^{-8}$, so one expects at least one such event only once there are
$1/\nu = 2.5\times10^{7}$ cultures. Twenty-five million independent cultures is
not an experiment anyone will run; at $4000$ cultures the chance of sampling one
is $1.6\times10^{-4}$.

The implication is not that fluctuation tests fail, but that the
variance-to-mean ratio is the wrong summary of one. Its population value is
dominated by events no feasible experiment samples, so a published
variance-to-mean ratio is reporting the sample it got rather than the quantity
\eqref{eq:ldvar} describes. This is why the standard estimators
(Lea--Coulson, Ma--Sandri--Sarkar, maximum likelihood) fit the distribution's
shape instead: the shape is determined by the generations that \emph{are}
sampled, and the inference does not rest on the unsampled tail.

\subsection*{Section 7}

\paragraph{7.2}
Two errors. First, the categories overlap, so pathogenic-over-total is not a
proportion of anything: the denominator includes records also counted in the
numerator's complement, and the categories sum past the total. Second, even a
correctly computed proportion of \emph{submitted} records is not a probability
for a \emph{new} variant, because ClinVar is ascertainment-biased toward variants
someone had a reason to submit.

\subsection*{Section 8}

\paragraph{8.1}
Unrolling \eqref{eq:cgr}, $x_i = \sum_{k\ge0} 2^{-(k+1)} v_{b_{i-k}}$. The
coefficient of $v_{b_{i-k}}$ is $2^{-(k+1)}$. If two positions share their last
$k$ bases, the difference of their embeddings is supported on terms of index
$> k$, hence bounded by $\sum_{j>k} 2^{-(j+1)}\mathrm{diam} = 2^{-(k+1)}\mathrm{diam}$.

\paragraph{8.2}
At the filtration limit the complex is connected for any sufficiently dense
sample of a connected attractor, so $\beta_0 = 1$ carries no information about
the sequence. It becomes informative only at $\epsilon$ below the typical
nearest-neighbour spacing, where $\beta_0$ counts clusters --- and then it is
measuring sampling density, not biology.

\subsection*{Section 9}

\paragraph{9.1}
Theorem~\ref{thm:trade} with $\Delta\Tw = -1$ gives $\Delta\Wr = +1$. Physically
the compensating writhe appears as a crossing of the axis --- a supercoil --- in
the flanking DNA.

\paragraph{9.2}
$\Lk$ counts signed crossings of two closed curves and is an integer for
topological reasons. $\Tw$ and $\Wr$ are integrals and individually real; their
sum is constrained to be the integer $\Lk$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:cwf}. If the
ribbon is not closed, $\Lk$ is undefined and the decomposition fails --- which is
why a nicked plasmid has no superhelical density.

\paragraph{9.3}
$95$ turns per $1000$ bp at $50$ bp/s is $95 \times (50/1000) = 4.75$ turns per
second.

\subsection*{Section 10}

\paragraph{10.1}
$\frac{1}{5}\cdot\frac{62500}{9} = \frac{62500}{45} = \frac{12500}{9}$. Verifying
it confirms a property of the model, and the model has been refuted; a correct
consequence of a false premise is not evidence for anything.

\paragraph{10.3}
Claims that would change: anything deriving a burden or rate from stage counts.
Claims that would not: the live database records of Section~\ref{sec:variants},
the topology of Section~\ref{sec:dnatop}, the clinical decision rule. The
distinguishing feature is whether the claim takes the mechanism as an input or
measures its consequence directly.

\subsection*{Section 11}

\paragraph{11.1}
Nesting means each null preserves everything the previous one preserved, plus
one more level of structure. A signal that survives \textbf{mono} but dies
against \textbf{dinuc} is therefore \emph{located}: it is dinucleotide
composition and nothing above it. Three unrelated nulls would each reject or
fail to reject, with no way to attribute the difference, because they would
differ in several respects at once. Nesting converts a set of yes/no answers
into a decomposition.

A trinucleotide-preserving null would separate codon-level composition from
codon \emph{order}: the \textbf{codon} null already preserves usage, so a
trinucleotide null that ignores frame would isolate overlapping-triplet
statistics, such as the depletion of \texttt{CpG}-containing triplets, from the
reading frame itself.

\paragraph{11.2}
The two-sided $p$ of $0.099$ counts null draws at least as extreme as the
observation \emph{in either direction}. A one-sided $p$ of $0.95$ for ``greater''
is the same data read as though we had predicted the direction in advance. We
had not: Section~\ref{sec:tda} expected, if anything, extra structure, so the
observed deficit was a surprise. Choosing the tail after seeing which way the
data fell means the rejection region was not fixed before the experiment, and
the nominal error rate no longer describes the procedure actually followed.

Pre-registering it here is easy, because the scientific question has a
direction: the hypothesis under test was that the real sequence carries
\emph{more} topological structure than composition explains, so the
pre-registered test is one-sided ``greater'', and it fails resoundingly
($p \approx 0.95$). Reporting the two-sided value is the more conservative
choice and is what the section does.

\paragraph{11.3}
A within-domain shuffle preserves the position of the domain in the sequence and
the amino-acid composition inside it, while destroying local motif order; it
therefore tests whether the \emph{motif} carries the alignment, with the
diagonal prior left intact. Reversing the yeast sequence preserves composition
and all dinucleotide-like local statistics read backwards, while breaking the
positional correspondence entirely; it therefore removes the diagonal prior but
also destroys homology, so a failure to align tells you less. The complementary
pair is informative: the within-domain shuffle isolates the motif, the reversal
isolates the diagonal.

\paragraph{11.4}
Theorem~\ref{thm:psr2} gives $\errate(\kept) \le \inserr$ for the independent
model. Multiplying both sides by $\Lambda \ge 0$ gives
$\errate(\kept)\Lambda \le \inserr\Lambda$, and the left side is
$\errate_\Lambda(\kept)$ by \eqref{eq:interact}. So PSR$_2$I is a one-line
corollary.

The Lean module proves it independently so that each statement can be cited
alone, and --- more importantly --- so that the hypothesis list of PSR$_2$I is
visible in its own right. Derived as a corollary, it would inherit whatever
PSR$_2$ assumes without restating it, and the point of the exercise is that
$\Lambda \ge 0$ is the only new hypothesis.

\paragraph{11.5}
The delivered load inverts when
$e_{\mathrm{mut}} s_{\mathrm{mut}} > e_{\mathrm{null}} s_{\mathrm{null}}$, i.e.\
when
\[
  \frac{s_{\mathrm{mut}}}{s_{\mathrm{null}}}
  \;>\; \frac{e_{\mathrm{null}}}{e_{\mathrm{mut}}}
  \;=\; \frac{155}{59} \;\approx\; 2.63 .
\]
So the variant would need to synthesise more than about $2.6$ times as much of
the genome as the exonuclease-null enzyme for the in vitro accuracy advantage to
be cancelled --- and more than $52 \times 2.63 \approx 137$ times as much to
account for the full in vivo ratio without any change in repair. The first
figure is not obviously absurd for a hyperactive enzyme; the second is.

The measurement is of replication-fork usage: what fraction of the genome is
copied by Pol~$\varepsilon$ rather than handed to Pol~$\delta$ or a translesion
polymerase, in each strain. Strand-specific sequencing of ribonucleotide-marked
or mutation-marked tracts would give it.

\paragraph{11.6}
A question where topology carries the argument needs the \emph{shape} of a data
set to be the object of interest, with no low-dimensional parameterisation
available --- for example whether a conformational ensemble from molecular
dynamics has two basins or one, where the answer is a Betti number and no
inequality about rates is relevant. A no-go theorem is useless there because
there is no parameterised model class to bound; the hypothesis is not ``this
mechanism'' but ``this geometry''.

The structural feature that decides it: no-go theorems apply when the hypothesis
space is a family of models closed under parameter choice, so a bound uniform
over the family can be proved. Topology applies when the hypothesis concerns an
invariant of an object, and the object is given as a point cloud rather than as
a model.
