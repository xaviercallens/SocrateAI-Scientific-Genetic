## Edition 3 result: stage-removal models refuted
`formal/ANSE/ProofreadingStageRemoval.lean` proves that if an error rate is an insertion error times one escape factor in [0,1] per proofreading stage, then no stage-removal mutant can exceed the stage-free (exonuclease-null) rate (`PSR_2`), for any stage count and any per-stage factors. A mutant observed at rho times exonuclease-null must have an insertion error at least rho times wild type (`PSR_3`).

Tested against Kane & Shcherbakova 2014 (Cancer Res 74:1895, Table 2), where `pol2-P301R` (the yeast analogue of POLE P286R) and the exonuclease-null `pol2-4` are measured in the same table and the same isogenic mismatch-repair-proficient background:

| Allele | CAN1 fold over WT | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `pol2-4` (exonuclease-null) | 2.9x | the `PSR_2` ceiling |
| `pol2-P301R` (POLE P286R) | 153x | **breaks the ceiling by 52.3x** (>=35.3x on published 95% CIs) |

Three further cancer alleles break it (P286H, F367S, S459F). All four controls pass; V411L and six neutral alleles are correctly not flagged. Xing 2019 shows P301R is *more accurate* than exonuclease-null in the purified system without mismatch repair (59 vs 155 x10^-6), so the ordering reverses by 137x in the cell and intrinsic error production cannot be the explanatory variable. Honest tension retained in the paper: `pol2-P301R msh6-delta` is synthetic lethal while `pol2-4 msh6-delta` is viable, which argues against complete mismatch-repair evasion.

Provenance: `data/pole_proofreading_literature.json` (verbatim quotes + URLs, read from primary full text), `scripts/run_proofreading_stage_removal_test.py`, `data/proofreading_stage_removal_test.json`, figures from `scripts/make_proofreading_figures.py`, regression tests in `tests/test_proofreading_stage_removal.py`.
